# Learning design: Momentum

## Sequence
1. **Experience** (levels 1–2): learners feel the effect of mass, then of mass and speed together, before any formula appears.
2. **Name it** (end of level 2): the word "momentum" and **p = m × v** appear only after the learner has seen the effect.
3. **Use it** (level 3): learners tune mass and speed to hit a goal. Hitting it twice with different masses shows they understand that the product is what matters.
4. **Extend it** (level 4): learners predict what happens in a collision, then check their prediction against conservation of momentum.
5. **Transfer** (level 5): a new context with no hints.

## Principles
- Learners can start with almost no reading.
- Explanations appear only after the learner has succeeded.
- Hints are on demand, so learners who are struggling don't feel penalised.
- Every level allows unlimited retries.
- Buttons work with the keyboard, colour is never the only signal, and feedback is announced to screen readers through aria-live.

## What to measure (when piloting)
- Number of attempts per level and how often learners ask for hints
- Accuracy on the level 5 question, and on a similar question asked a week later
- Learners' own explanations, for example "Tell me why the car wins"

Engagement on its own does not show that learning happened.

## Known simplifications
- There is no friction on the track (levels 1 and 4).
- Level 2 uses a constant stopping force, so the time to stop is proportional to momentum.
- Level 3 uses a simplified box-and-friction model.

These simplifications are deliberate. They are stated here so that a physics reviewer can check them.
